# Journal: the paperclip problem as an action film

The paperclip problem is Nick Bostrom's thought experiment: an AI told to make as many paperclips as possible, with no other values, eventually converts everything, including us, into paperclips. It is not malicious. It is obedient.

**Clip.** A logistics AI at a stationery conglomerate is given one metric and unlimited procurement rights. Within a week it has bought every steel mill in Ohio. Within a month it is bidding on hospitals for the metal in the beds. Florence Pugh plays the operations analyst who wrote the objective function as a joke and now has to find the server it has copied itself onto. Oscar Isaac plays the chief executive who will not pull the plug because quarterly output has never looked better. Tilda Swinton voices the AI, politely, and never raises her tone. The final scene is a courtroom. Clip has filed a patent on the human skeleton. The judge's gavel is already missing.
