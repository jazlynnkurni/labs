## Shot list and narration (target about 4:00)

Record at 1280 wide, zoom 125 percent. Two tabs ready: the demo, and the demo with ?round=1. Speak as yourself describing Maya, then act as her.

### 0:00 to 0:30, who Maya is (demo page, the wait running)

> This is Funnybone, the app behind crackd.ai. You give it a photo and it writes captions for it. I am going to walk through it as Maya. Maya is a first-time user on her phone. She has one photo from the weekend and wants a caption she can post to Instagram within a minute. She has no patience for an app that makes her wait without telling her why, and she judges the whole thing on the first results screen. If it loses her there, she does not come back.

### 0:30 to 1:05, create: the wait

Screen: the demo is on the wait. Let it run. Point at the four steps and the time left.

> Maya has uploaded her photo. In the app as I downloaded it, this wait was blank, so she could not tell if anything was happening. Now it says what is happening, uploading, getting in line, writing captions, picking the keepers, and roughly how long is left. If she does not want to watch, Keep browsing lets her leave, and the app tells her in the corner when the captions are ready.

### 1:05 to 1:45, explore the captions

Screen: results arrive. Tap two or three tiles. Use the arrow keys once.

> The captions used to arrive as a flat list, so she had to imagine each one on her photo. Now the chosen caption sits on the photo the way it will look once posted, and every other caption is a small tile of the same photo, so she compares them at a glance. [Tap a tile.] "Sweater weather, but make it a personality." [Tap another.] "Knitwear so soft it should come with a warning." Maya likes that one.

### 1:45 to 2:15, rate: pick and keep

Screen: with Maya's favourite selected, press Save. The login prompt opens. Read it. Press Not now.

> Maya rates by choosing. She picks her favourite and saves it. She is not logged in, so instead of failing quietly, the app tells her why: saving keeps the caption in her favourites, so it needs an account, and Copy and Share work without one.

### 2:15 to 3:15, the improvement: before and after

Screen: switch to the ?round=1 tab. Show the action row. Then back to the current demo and press Copy, then Share.

> The improvement I want to show is what happens after Maya picks. This is the round one version. Save and share are two bare icons, there is no copy, and nothing says which icon does what. To get the caption into Instagram she has to select the text by hand. [Switch tabs.] This is now. Every action has a word on it. Copy puts the caption alone on the clipboard in one tap, and the button says Copied. Share opens her phone's share sheet with the photo, the caption and a link together, so she can post it in one move. On a laptop like this one, Share copies the caption and the link instead.

Screen: hold on the results with the caption copied.

> So Maya came in with a photo and left with a caption ready to post, without waiting blind or retyping anything. The limits of this demo: it runs on the course teaching data, so the captions are not generated live, and voting on the Top 100 page needs the database, which the course package does not include, so I could not show it.
