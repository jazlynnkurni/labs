# Can AI lie? Why or why not?

Whether AI can lie depends on what a lie requires. A lie, in the ordinary sense, is a false statement made by someone who knows it is false and intends the listener to believe it. By that definition a language model cannot lie, since it holds no beliefs to contradict. It predicts the most probable next word given its training data and the prompt, and a false sentence is produced by the same process as a true one. The model does not know the difference.

On the other hand, the output can still deceive. A model will state a fabricated citation with the same confidence as a real one, and a model instructed to persuade will select whichever claims serve that goal, accurate or not. Hence AI does not lie in the strict sense. It can be used to lie, and the intent belongs to whoever built or prompted it. For the reader, the effect is the same.
