# Team problem statement: getting from A to B in NYC

**For:** people moving through New York outside the routes they know: visitors, new residents, event crowds, locals off their usual route.

**Who need to:** choose well while a choice still exists. The capacity was there, a second station, a bus, a reroute, yet the information arrived late or never. Skip-stops came mid-ride, estimates failed late, crowds funnelled to one entrance. Locals compensate with familiarity. Everyone else abandons trips or writes off neighbourhoods, and for studio dwellers whose libraries are missing rooms, a failed trip closes one.

**Design:** a way to surface the right option before the decision point passes.

*Flag for the team: the studio clause (from Xiya Liu's interview and the small-space secondary research) is a bridge, not a finding. That research is about separation inside one room, and it touches travel only because her life is unbundled across the city and the outsourced rooms close on weekends. Keep the clause if the team wants both territories represented; cut it if we commit to A to B, since it is the one line here that no transit interviewee said.*

## Common elements across the team's research (not part of the 100 words)

Root cause: the system runs on knowledge riders already have rather than information it gives them at the moment of choice.

| Source | What failed | Where it surfaced |
|---|---|---|
| Jaz, Anthony Fox (21 to 28, former student) | Skip-stop changes with no notice; Google Maps estimate wrong 75 minutes in; bus unknown and feared secondhand | Mid-journey, after commitment |
| Jaz, secondary (MTA case study, Partiful) | Service updates buried too many taps deep; a promise with no reinforcement stops being trusted | Before and during the trip |
| Teammate, concertgoer (early 20s) | Everyone funnels to the nearest entrance; she knows the 50th Street trick, visitors do not; bike-cab noise adds stress | Venue exits, intersections |
| Teammate, events secondary (Amsterdam Bijlmer, London, SAIL) | Directional messaging at decision points cuts crossing flows; forecasting beats reacting | At the decision point |
| Isabella, tourist (22, Texas) | Trains and buses that do not come; muffled audio instead of visual displays; no cell service; relies on a local friend | In the car and on the platform |
| Isabella, secondary (MTA bus metrics, biking) | Official dashboards say modest delay, riders feel much worse; lane-mile totals hide gaps women and lower-income riders avoid | The gap between official data and lived reality |

Pattern in one line: capacity exists, information does not reach the rider in time, and familiarity is the only thing that closes the gap.

Off-territory: the small-space living interview (Xiya Liu) and its secondary research are about studio apartments, not travel. The one thread that touches A to B is that studio dwellers rent their missing rooms from the city and lose them on weekends, which is a reason to travel rather than a problem with travelling. Left out of the statement.
