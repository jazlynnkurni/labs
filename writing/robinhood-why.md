# Why Robinhood? (APM behavioural answer, spoken, about 90 seconds)

Robinhood was the first place I understood what owning a share of something actually meant.

The reason that line matters is a summer at MUFG in Jakarta. The brief was to merge a bank, an insurer and a vehicle financing arm into one app, and I tested the concepts in branch offices with unbanked users. I took six pillars to the stakeholders. Four survived. One was cut for low ROI and one because it clashed with a subsidiary's KPIs, and nothing a user had said came into either decision. MUFG builds for people who already know how a bank works. The people in those branches did not, and nobody was building for them.

That is the part of Robinhood I care about. Democratising finance is the line on the website, but what convinced me is what shipped this year. Strategies caps the management fee. Banking gives Gold members things that used to need a private banker. Agentic trading launched in May and 150,000 customers have opened accounts for it since. These are products for someone who is starting without the vocabulary.

Financial freedom is still a dream a lot of Indonesians are chasing, my family included. I am first generation. I want to build for the people who do not have the knowledge I happened to pick up.

And the APM seat specifically, because the programme is still being worked out and the posting is direct about mentorship from senior product leaders. I would rather help figure out what the programme is than inherit one that is already settled.

In short, the opportunity to contribute, be a part of something meaningful, and continuously learn from the people around me is one that is hard to pass.
