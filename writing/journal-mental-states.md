# Journal: mental states and AI policy

If we accept that an AI system can hold beliefs and intentions, the law changes first, since contract, fraud and criminal law all turn on what someone meant to do. A chatbot that gives false advice could then be said to have deceived the customer, rather than its developer having shipped a defective product. The closest precedent is the corporation, which has no mind yet is assigned intent and fined as a single entity. A model cannot be fined, so its liability would flow back to its owner anyway.

The natural proponents would be large developers, since locating intent in the system moves responsibility away from them, and insurers, who could price a system with a stable character. Consumer advocates and tort lawyers would oppose it for the same reason. My own view is that mental states are a useful description and a poor foundation. Responsibility should follow control and benefit.
