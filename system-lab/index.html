The caps question

Your friend's first tell. Today a case study carries 23 to 26 caps labels at 10.5px with .15em tracking, one above nearly every heading. Set in a row they read as a template, because that is exactly what generated pages do: a small spaced label above a title, every time.

  1. The eyebrow lives in the page head only. One per page, the same spec on every page, as your head rule already says. It names the page type and the subject.
  2. Section labels become sentence case. 13px, 500, ink2, no tracking. "Final thoughts", not "FINAL THOUGHTS". Where the label only restates the heading, drop it and let the heading stand.
  3. Two weights per face. Jakarta 500 and 600. Helvetica 400. The font link drops 700 (loaded, never used) and the five 300s go to 400.
  4. Nine sizes, not fifteen. 10.5, 12.5, 13, 15.5, 17, 22 and the two clamps. 10, 12, 14, 14.5, 15, 26 and 32 each snap to the nearest role.
  5. Measure. Body 52ch, lede 56ch, h1 22ch. Heads balance, body is pretty, changing digits are tabular.

Radius

Pills for every one-line control, 20 for the plate, 12 for what sits inside it, 8 for a tile, square for everything that is not an object. Five values, never animated.

The golden rule, and the one under it

Every source that is worth reading agrees on two things. One: nested corners are concentric, so the outer radius equals the inner radius plus the padding between them, and a corner gets smaller as you go in, never bigger. Two: radius scales with the element, so a one-line control is a pill and a large plate carries a larger radius than a small tile. Your own code already states the first in a comment: "outer = inner + padding. 14 + 18 = 32." The system makes it the law.

Why not all rounded

A radius says "this is an object you could pick up". Give it to the things that are: a button, a photo on a plate, a card in a deck. Text is not an object, a number is not an object, a section is not an object. Round them and the page becomes a tray of pebbles, which is the second tell your friend named. Today the site has 17 distinct radii; the case studies site has 18 and 26 with !important. Five is enough.

  1. Square unless it is pressed or it holds a picture. Headings, paragraphs, stat cells, tables, code, figure captions and section rules have no box and so no radius.
  2. Concentric or nothing. Inner = outer − padding, floored at 8. If the maths lands under 8, the inner element goes square and the outer carries the corner alone.
  3. Proportion. Pill for anything one line tall. 20 for plates wider than about 200. 8 for tiles under about 120. Nothing in between gets a value of its own.
  4. Three radii per screen at most, and the same role always gets the same value. The pill on the nav and the pill on a tag are the same pill.
  5. All four corners the same. Mixed corners belong to physical objects only: the phone shell on About (18 and 30) and the sheet whose bottom edge is joined to the screen.
  6. Never animate a radius. The cursor's border-radius transition and the nav drop's go. A shape that changes shape reads as goo.
  7. Plain corners on the web. No corner smoothing, no squircle libraries. Continuous corners are iOS's, and the Clover app gets them from the system, never hand-faked.
  8. Outline rides the radius. The 1px 10% edge is drawn on the same shape (inset shadow or border-radius: inherit), never on a wrapper with a different radius.

Where the shadows are today

Twenty distinct box-shadows. Nine of them are on the custom cursor in its states and themes, three are card hovers, one is the sheet, one the nav, the rest the phone mock. The shadows your friend saw are mostly the cursor and the hover lift following the pointer around. Remove those and the site reads as paper again.

  1. Shadow means floating. Only something that sits above other content earns one: the nav pill, the sheet, a menu. Plates, cards in the list, stat cells and images rest flat.
  2. Two recipes. lift: 0 1px 2px .04, 0 6px 20px .07 (the pill shadow already in the code). float: 0 20px 70px .22. In dark, lift deepens to .3 / .35 because dark paper swallows a light shadow.
  3. Hover never adds a shadow. A hover answers with the outline darkening to 16%, or a 2px lift in transform, not a glow spreading under the card. The 18px 40px hover on the Marks cards goes.
  4. The cursor casts no shadow. See Motion for whether it exists at all.
  5. Glass only in iOS. No backdrop blur on web plates. The nav's blur over scrolling content is the one exception because the pill genuinely floats.

Where the time goes today

Thirty transition recipes across the three main pages, with durations from 160ms to 800ms and two spring curves. The pointer is followed by a custom cursor that resizes, recolours, grows a label and casts a shadow, every page hides the system pointer with cursor: none !important, the hero discs are draggable, and copy fades in on scroll. Each is fine alone. Together they are what "slow and overwhelming" means: the page keeps answering before the reader asked.

Rules

  1. One ambient motion per page. On the home page that is the discs. On a case study it is nothing, the media is the motion.
  2. Content that is there. No fade-in on scroll for text, headings, stats or figures. The reveal observers come out of the case studies and About. A video may start when it enters view; that is playback, not a reveal.
  3. The system pointer comes back. cursor: none goes. The labelled cursor survives in one place if at all: over media plates that carry data-cur, as a plain label with no shadow, no resize and no spring. Everywhere else the browser's arrow and hand.
  4. Hover answers once, in 160. Colour, outline or a 2px lift. Not shadow, not scale, not a label sliding in. Gated behind (hover: hover) so phones never see it.
  5. Drag is for the discs. No other object on the site is draggable. The deck pages by click or key.
  6. Springs are for things with mass. The overshoot curve stays on the discs and the phone crate. The nav drop, the cursor, the Marks cards and the About cells move on the plain ease.
  7. Keyboard actions do not animate. Tab, escape and enter land instantly.
  8. Nothing animates from scale(0), and nothing reveals text by growing width. Opacity and a small slide.
  9. Reduced motion zeroes every duration. The tokens file already does this; the pages inherit it.
  10. Budget check before shipping a page: count the things that move without a click. If the number is above one, cut until it is one.
