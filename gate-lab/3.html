At a glance

Manus was growing faster than its users could keep up with.

Meta acquired Manus AI during the winter break I spent there as its only product designer. Every release that made the agent smarter also made it harder for everyday users to understand. By the time Manus 1.5 Max launched the product could do far more than most users knew how to ask for. The community site that should have closed that gap had become a static archive nobody navigated.

I led the end-to-end redesign that turned the Manus Community from a static archive of content into an AI-guided learning system that grows with both the product and its users.

Problem

Every new Manus feature made the product harder for new users to start with.

Manus was becoming one of the most capable AI agents on the market while non-technical users struggled to tell where to begin, what the agent could do for them and how to take part in the community around the product.

The existing community site had been built for a simpler product and held past events and content with no pathway or progression to tell a new user where to start.

Solution preview

User lands on the community

User explores personalized recommendations

Users grow alongside Manus

Initial pivot

Our first two approaches both failed usability testing.

Our first framing →

We first assumed the problem was motivation and built toward gamification in the belief that points, levels and quests would get people engaging.

Why the first approach failed →

Usability testing showed that gamification overwhelmed people because it added friction on top of a system they already found unclear. Participants were confused by the navigation and the role progression and could not say what the game mechanics were for. We therefore pivoted to a simpler community system built around what users needed.

*I built the early prototype in Lovable to get usability feedback fast.

User interviews (13) and secondary research →

  • Non-technical users could not identify what Manus could do for them specifically.
  • The community felt like a static archive that users left within minutes of arriving.
  • Users wanted to contribute but had no clear way to share their work.

Understanding our users →

Thirteen participants in two groups: 5 community newcomers and 8 power users.

Community newcomer

Age 22–35

  • Doesn't know where to start or what Manus can do for them.
  • Wants to learn fast and find opportunities worth their time.

Power user

Age 25–40+

  • Has no structured way to show or share their work.
  • Wants their contributions seen and credited by the community.
Finding the gaps in the market

Each existing kind of community failed users in a different way.

Discord and chat communities

  • Fast and informal
  • Knowledge gets lost in the scroll
  • No lasting record of contributions

Gamified builder platforms

  • Engagement spikes and then fades
  • Rewards activity over impact
  • High cognitive load

Traditional forums and docs

  • Structured and searchable
  • Low participation and slow replies
  • Not built for fast product updates
Our direction

How might we make AI capabilities legible to everyday users?

How might we turn passive members into active contributors?

Development

We iterated toward a system that could grow with Manus.

The problem with the static version →

A third round of usability testing on the internally launched static experience found that 7 out of 10 users disengaged quickly. Where they lingered before leaving told us more than the fact that they left.

Interactive elements →

Users spent far less time on the role selector and the three-card feature section than on the Global Distribution map and the Next Hackathon countdown. The pulsing map and the live countdown were the only two elements on the page that moved.

Takeaway →

The test showed that the information architecture needed realigning and that users were drawn to elements that felt live and relevant to them. That finding led me straight into another round of iteration aimed at longer time on page.

Testing

Testing the personalized path.

Adjustment to the personalized path #1 →

Across 8 usability tests the "Start My AI-Guided Path" CTA had the highest click-through of any version. For this version I borrowed from RPG games by treating each user as a character who moves through the community with their own stats, focus areas and progression mode.

The founder especially liked the "Powered by Manus" tag because it showed the product's own intelligence working inside the community.

Adjustment to the personalized path #2 →

After I presented to my PM she told me the team planned to add more community roles. The original fixed layout of three cards held one card per featured opportunity and would break the moment the community team launched a fourth role.

To avoid a manual design update for every new role such as Campus Leader, Ambassador or Regional Hub I redesigned the recommendations layer to work for any role. The "Powered by Manus" inference layer now surfaces whatever is relevant to each user whether that is a hackathon, an ambassador program or a role that does not exist yet.

Mapping the journey with the PM and engineers →

Engineering, the community team, the PM and design worked from one shared flow so that every opportunity the inference layer surfaced had an owner and an API behind it.

Solution

An AI-guided community system that adapts as users grow.

01

Centralized community dashboard →

The dashboard gives every Manus user one home for what they need to know and do. Users land on a screen that shows their role and active path alongside upcoming events, pending contributions and reminders for anything that needs their attention.

02

AI-guided personalized paths →

"Your Path, Powered by Manus" infers each user's role and focus area from their behavior and suggests a next step for that person. In place of a generic feed users see "Recommended this week. Prioritized by fit, not engagement." with a "Why this matters" note that explains every recommendation.

03

Recommendations that work for any role →

When users log into Manus the inference layer surfaces matching opportunities that range from hackathons and ambassador programs to campus leadership and roles that do not exist yet. This lets the community team add roles without any new design.

After rollout users named the personalized paths, the contextual recommendations and the "Why this matters" explanations as their main reasons to stay. 75% called the experience more personally relevant than the static version.

Final thoughts

Key takeaways and next steps.

  1. Clear guidance and a simple structure kept people in the community where engagement mechanics did not.
  2. Letting research kill our first two concepts early is what led us to the right solution.
  3. The most important design decision is asking "so what?" before users have to ask it themselves.
